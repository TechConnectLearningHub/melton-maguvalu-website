The complete tradition

Eight chapters.
One continuous story.

01 Chapter 1 of 8

The floral identity of Telangana

Bathukamma is the Festival of Flowers and a unique, colourful celebration of Telangana. It represents the cultural identity of the region through a flower stack arranged with seasonal blooms—many traditionally valued for their medicinal qualities—in seven concentric layers shaped like a clay cone.

02 Chapter 2 of 8

Mother Goddess Come Alive

In Telugu, “Bathukamma” means “Mother Goddess Come Alive”. Goddess Maha Gauri Devi—the Life Giver—is worshipped in the form of Bathukamma, turning the floral arrangement into both sacred symbol and living expression of womanhood.

03 Chapter 3 of 8

A week of songs and gathering

The festival is celebrated for one whole week. Women make small Bathukammas, gather around them each evening, sing, play and later immerse them in a nearby pond. The final day, Saddula Bathukamma, is the grand finale.

04 Chapter 4 of 8

Flowers gathered by the household

On the final day, men from the household traditionally gather flowers such as Celosia, Senna auriculata, Tagetes, Chrysanthemum, Nelumbo nucifera, Cucumis sativus, Memecylon edule, Tridax procumbens, Trachyspermum ammi, Katla and Teku. Families then sit together to build the large Bathukamma.

05 Chapter 5 of 8

Women form the circle

Women dress in traditional attire with jewellery and accessories, place Bathukamma in the courtyard and gather in a wide circle. They sing while moving around it repeatedly, creating a human circle of unity, love and sisterhood.

06 Chapter 6 of 8

Procession, immersion and return

Before dusk, women carry Bathukammas on their heads in a colourful procession towards a larger water body. Folklore songs fill the streets. After more singing and playing, the Bathukammas are immersed, homemade sweets are shared, and women return home singing songs of praise.

07 Chapter 7 of 8

Earth, water and renewal

Bathukamma celebrates the relationship between earth, water and human beings. During the preceding week, women also make Boddemma—a form of Gowri, or Mother Durga, shaped from earth—and immerse it in the pond. This practice traditionally reinforces the pond and supports water retention.

08 Chapter 8 of 8

A celebration rooted in ecology

Bathukamma is regarded as an environmentally friendly festival. Flowers with medicinal value are immersed in water and are traditionally understood to help purify the pond and improve the surrounding environment. The celebration honours nature, collective spirit, women’s strength and the resource-conscious knowledge of agrarian communities.
